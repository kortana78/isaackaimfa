Inspiration Behind the Wishverse

26 June 2026

The inspiration behind the Wishverse Framework surprisingly came from internet memes about wishes. I kept seeing jokes such as "I wish for more wishes," "I wish I could wish again," sometimes "I wish nobody else could make wishes." Although they were intended as jokes, they made me wonder whether there was a deeper structure hidden beneath them.

My idea was simple. What if we formally constructed a universe governed by rules for wishing? What kinds of wishes would be possible? Which wishes would be impossible? How would wishes about other wishes behave? Could such a system be studied mathematically and logically? These questions became the starting point of the Wishverse Framework.

I honestly do not know where this research program will ultimately lead, but I have been surprised by how much I have learned simply by trying to formalize the concept of wishing. Along the way, I have discovered unexpected connections to logic, mathematics, philosophy, computer science, and formal systems.

At the time of writing, I have completed two papers. The first introduces the framework and has received valuable feedback from several renowned researchers in logic and analytic philosophy. I am currently preparing a revised version that addresses many of the suggestions they raised. If you are reading Version 2 of the paper, then those revisions have already been incorporated.

The second paper introduces several new concepts and develops the framework in a much more mathematically rigorous way. It has been submitted to a journal, and I look forward to seeing how it is received. While it has not yet undergone the same level of review as the introductory paper, I hope it encourages further discussion and exploration.

There is still an enormous amount of work to be done. I have yet to develop an algebraic classification of wishverse structures, nor have I completed a modal logical embedding of the framework into existing formal systems. Each new question seems to open several more, making this one of the most enjoyable research projects I have undertaken.

The journey has only just begun and there is still plenty of juicy mathematics and philosophy waiting to be explored.
