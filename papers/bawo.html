The State-Space Complexity of Malawian Bawo: A Combinatorial Upper Bound

Preprint    2026
Abstract Bawo is the Malawian variant of the four-row mancala game Bao, played competitively by the Yao. While the Zanzibar (Swahili) form of Bao has received sustained attention in computational game theory, the Malawian variant has not, to our knowledge, been formally specified or measured. This paper takes a first step. We give a finite-state model of Bawo, separate the configuration space from the legal and reachable state spaces, and derive the exact cardinality of the configuration space, which upper-bounds both. The bound is approximately 1.7 × 1026 configurations, and exactly half this, about 8.7 × 1025, once a conditionally stated player-swap symmetry is quotiented out. This figure is an upper bound only: it places Bawo no higher than the chess range and well below Go, but because most configurations are unreachable under legal play it leaves the lower end of Bawo's true position count open, pending a reachability estimate that we specify but do not yet carry out. We are careful to distinguish what is unconditional (the configuration count) from what depends on a not-yet-formalised rule set (position adequacy, transition totality, and symmetry invariance), and we set out the empirical methodology by which the reachable state space, the quantity of ultimate interest, is to be estimated. All figures are reproducible from the short script in Appendix A.
