The Wishverse Under Adversarial Granting

How Coarse Specification Breaks the Sufficiency of a Single Wish    2026
Abstract A wish is informally an operator that receives a state of the world and produces a different one. Modeled naively as a non-deterministic map w: S → 𝒫(S), a wish is just a binary relation, and the theory reduces to abstract rewriting. We recover the missing structure by separating three ingredients the naive model conflates: feasibility (what a granting agent could deliver), preference (what the wisher wants), and expressibility (the language in which wishes can be phrased). The residual non-determinism of the naive map is then granter discretion, resolved adversarially against the preference: the formal monkey’s paw. In this static, order-theoretic setting we first recover, as reformulations, two known impossibility results from the theory of misspecified objectives: that nontrivial betrayal-free specification is impossible over rich wish languages (an order-theoretic counterpart of the unhackability results of Skalse et al.), and that a wish language too coarse to isolate the optimum makes every expressible wish betrayable (a bounded counterpart of the incomplete-proxy result of Zhuang and Hadfield-Menell). Our new contribution is sequential. We introduce the wishing game, prove a single-cell reduction showing the wisher’s only power is to confine the granter to one language cell, and establish the paper’s central result: when feasibility is transitive, one wish is optimal for every utility if and only if the language is fine enough to separate each state’s successors, and otherwise multi-step wishing is strictly more valuable, by the full unit-bounded margin, even though the feasibility relation is transitive. The mechanism is not intransitivity of any quotient relation, but the granter’s freedom inside a coarse cell. Betrayal and the value of sequential wishing are two faces of one operator inequality. The exact gap condition is settled: a sequential-value gap exists precisely when forced reachability outruns one-shot securability, a reachability-game winning region decidable in polynomial time.
