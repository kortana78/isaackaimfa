Epistemic Blindness to Memory Loss and the Fragility of Personal Identity

2026
Abstract This paper examines a fundamental paradox at the heart of cognitive science and the philosophy of personal identity. If the loss of memory in an individual is subjectively undetectable at the moment it occurs, then no individual possesses a reliable internal mechanism for verifying the continuity of the self. Building on Lockean memory theory, Schechtman’s narrative identity framework, and clinical evidence from confabulation and anosognosia, the paper argues that the conventional assumption — that we know who we are because we remember who we were — is epistemically untenable. If we cannot detect the erasure of our own memories, and if the brain actively fabricates coherent narratives to conceal these gaps, then personal identity is not a verifiable archive but an unverifiable present-tense performance.
